A Catholic Apostolate for Integral Ecology

We form stewards of our common home.

An invitation to contemplate creation, transform the heart, and turn faith into a concrete way of caring, serving and evangelising.

Monterrey, Nuevo León, Mexico.

Discover the path The Lumen Terrae Retreat

What if caring for creation were also a way of living our faith?

What does it really mean that the world is creation?
What place does the human person hold within it?
What have we received?
What are we called to keep?
And what has to change in us first, so that we can transform what surrounds us?

Lumen Terrae comes from a simple and deep conviction: the crisis of our common home is also a call to conversion of heart.

I

We are not simply an environmental project

What Lumen Terrae is, and where it looks from.

Lumen Terrae is a Catholic apostolate for integral ecology. We help people, families and communities discover creation as a gift of God, and understand that care for our common home is part of a Christian life lived with coherence.

We bring together spirituality, formation, community and concrete action to accompany a path that begins in contemplation and leads toward conversion, care, service and witness.

We are not only trying to change habits. We are trying to transform the way of seeing that produces them.
II

The outward crisis also reveals an inward one

Why Lumen Terrae begins inside rather than outside.

We have learned to relate to many things through the logic of possession, consumption and usefulness. We consume without always asking what we need. We use without always asking what we have received. We desire without always asking what makes us truly free.

And we often speak of caring for the world without stopping to ask what needs to be transformed in us first.

Relationship with God
Do we recognise God as Creator?
Relationship with goods
Do we live as absolute owners, or as stewards of what we have received?
Relationship with others
Do we understand that caring for our common home also means caring for the dignity and good of our brothers and sisters?
III

The answer does not begin outside. It begins in the heart

Before acting, we contemplate. Before transforming, we convert.

Before speaking of care, we learn to give thanks. Before wanting to change the world, we learn to recognise what God has entrusted to us.

Contemplate
Recognise the gift.
Convert
Order the heart.
Care
Transform the way we live.
Serve
Place that conversion at the service of the common good.
Evangelise
Make one's own life a witness.
IV

Our common home is more than the environment

What integral ecology means, without the jargon.

Integral ecology invites us to recognise that everything is connected. The way we treat creation is bound up with the way we live, consume, work, produce, relate and serve. Which is why care for our common home cannot be separated from human dignity, the family, community, the economy, culture and the common good.

Creation
We receive it as a gift.
Person
We recognise their dignity.
Community
We build relationships of care.
Common good
We seek the good we share.
Common home
We keep what has been entrusted to us.

This is not only about caring for the place where we live. It is about learning to live in a way worthy of what we have received.

And we do not separate care for creation from care for people. Which is why we pay particular attention to those who experience most directly the consequences of environmental degradation, poverty, vulnerability and exclusion. We do not care for the earth in order to forget the human person: we care for our common home because all of us live in it.

V

What is a steward?

Someone who understands that what they have received is not absolutely their own.

Someone who contemplates before consuming. Discerns before deciding. Gives thanks before demanding. Cares before discarding. Serves before claiming. And who understands that their decisions can become a concrete way of loving God and neighbour.

You do not need to be an environmentalist. You need to be willing to convert.
Contemplates
Learns to recognise the beauty and the gift of creation.
Gives thanks
Recognises having received before possessing.
Discerns
Examines decisions in the light of faith.
Lives with sobriety
Learns to distinguish between need, desire and excess.
Cares
Turns convictions into habits.
Serves
Places their abilities at the service of others.
Builds community
Understands that conversion also has a communal dimension.
Leads
Transforms what God has placed under their responsibility.
Evangelises
Makes their faith visible through the way they live.
VI

This is not a one-off talk. It is a path

Seven steps, and the experiences that sustain them.

Discover
That creation is a gift, and that our relationship with it can also express our faith.
Contemplate
To stop. To be silent. To look. To give thanks.
Convert
To recognise what needs to be put in order in our own life.
Care
To carry that conversion into our habits, decisions and relationships.
Serve
To place our abilities at the service of the common good.
Belong
To find a community that shares the same path.
Lead
To become agents of change in our family, parish, school, company or community.
The starting point
The Lumen Terrae Retreat

Every path begins by stopping. An experience of silence, contemplation, formation and conversion, to rediscover creation as a gift of God and to recognise our responsibility as stewards of our common home.

Stop
Step out of the noise for a while.
Contemplate
Learn to look at creation through faith.
Listen
Open space for God.
Discern
Recognise what needs to change.
Convert
Order your relationship with goods, consumption and creation.
Commit
Translate conversion into concrete action.

The retreat does not end when you leave. That is where the path begins.

Conversion needs a life that sustains it. Which is why the path continues after the retreat.

Formation
Courses, gatherings, content and experiences on integral ecology and the spirituality of creation.
Communities
Spaces where people and families go deeper, share, review habits, serve together and accompany one another. No one keeps this alone.
Everyday life
Habits and practices that carry conversion into the reality of ordinary days.
Missions
Concrete acts of care and restoration: to restore, to care, to educate, to serve, to accompany, to build.
Leadership
Forming people able to lead processes of transformation in their own communities.

Contemplation moves us. Conversion transforms us. And transformation sends us out. Because our common home is not an idea: it is the place where real people live.

VII

Lumen Terrae can live in your community

Where this path can take root, and who it is for.

Parishes
Integrating integral ecology into parish life and pastoral work.
Schools
Forming children, young people, teachers and families from a Christian vision of creation.
Families
Bringing ecological conversion into the home and into daily life.
Movements
Adding the spirituality of creation to existing processes of formation and community.
Religious communities
Deepening a spirituality of stewardship, sobriety and service.
Young people
Offering experiences of contemplation, formation, community and mission.
Is this path for you?

If you want to live your faith with greater coherence in daily life. If you are a family wanting to pass on to your children a relationship of gratitude, sobriety and responsibility. If you are young and looking for a living faith committed to the world you are called to transform. If you are a leader whose decisions affect others. If you are a community ready to move from concern to action.

VIII

Lumen Terrae is not

Where this apostolate draws its limits.

An environmental campaign.
A political movement.
An environmental NGO.
A green trend.
A collection of recycling tips.
A spirituality that confuses creation with God.
An activism that forgets the human person.
A replacement for catechesis, the parish or sacramental life.

It is a Catholic apostolate. And so Christ is at the centre.

We do not promise to change the world in a day.

We promise something more modest: to help you stop, to look, to give thanks, to recognise, to convert, to care, to serve — and to discover that one transformed life can be the beginning of a far greater transformation.
