About Mario Garza Livas and Metanoia Football

Who Mario is, what MFB is, and what connects the two.

Mario Garza Livas is a strategist specialized in the architecture and transformation of the professional football ecosystem. His expertise spans talent development and structuring, institutional club transformation, league governance and competitiveness, the design of national talent systems, and strategic analysis of the global football industry. Through Metanoia Football, he integrates strategy, intelligence, technology, and AI to approach football not as a collection of isolated organizations, but as an interdependent system in which talent, institutions, and governance determine competitive advantage.

Metanoia Football (MFB) is the football-industry vertical within The Metanoia Group, the institutional transformation firm Garza Livas founded and leads. MFB operates as a strategy and institutional-design think tank for football, dedicated to studying how football can better convert its talent, capabilities, and resources into sustainable performance, through better architectures of development, governance, representation, competition, and incentives.

In that role, MFB combines three complementary capabilities. As a think tank, it produces knowledge, applied research, frameworks, and proposals. As a strategy lab, it develops and explores conceptual models for understanding and solving football's complex problems, including the conversion of capacity into performance, the architecture of competitive advantage, development systems, player representation, and incentive design. And as an institutional design space, it carries that thinking forward into the design of better systems, structures, and governance mechanisms, rather than merely describing existing problems.

Under MFB's editorial imprint — and under his own name — Mario Garza Livas has developed a corpus of 24 applied research papers, organized into nine categories, that together form a strategic architecture for understanding, diagnosing, and transforming different levels of professional football: from talent development and training systems, to the institutional transformation of clubs, league and confederation governance, the representation and governance of the player's career, and the design of the rules and incentives of the competitive system itself.

No document in the corpus stands in isolation. All share the same technical vocabulary and the same underlying thesis: sustainable competitive advantage does not depend solely on talent or available resources, but on the institutional architecture capable of converting that potential into realized performance, reproducibly and over time.

The corpus therefore constitutes a research and design program applied to professional football: it studies how its systems work, identifies the mechanisms that explain its outcomes, and explores architectures capable of improving its performance, adaptation, and sustainability.

1 paper
The Double Helix of Talent

"The Double Helix of Talent" proposes a new way of understanding player development: not as a linear process based exclusively on training, but as the continuous interaction between deliberate learning and quality competitive exposure. Its central thesis holds that building capabilities and competing are not independent processes, but two engines that feed each other in a constant spiral: training builds capabilities, competition tests them in contexts of uncertainty and pressure, and the resulting evidence makes it possible to identify new learning needs and adapt the next development cycle.

Building on this architecture, the whitepaper develops a comprehensive model connecting talent, training, competition, pathways, measurement, and institutional governance. Its proposal shifts the focus away from the quantity of resources, training sessions, or minutes played, toward the system's real capacity to convert potential into adaptation and sustainable competitive performance.

Beyond the Academy

"Beyond the Academy" examines the case of Independiente del Valle to explore a broader question: how is a system built that can consistently and sustainably convert potential talent into professional players? The document proposes looking beyond the traditional academy and understanding development as an integrated chain of identification, recruitment, training, competition, adaptation, acceleration, mobility, and realization.

Drawing on concepts such as Competitive Density, Competitive Adaptation Capital, Meritocracy, Competitive Distance, and Marginal Learning Value, the text argues that real advantage lies not in accumulating talent, but in designing the conditions that force that talent to develop.

Download PDF
The Club as a Talent Refinery

"The Club as a Talent Refinery" proposes a new way of understanding the production of footballers: not as the isolated result of a good academy, but as the output of a comprehensive, measurable, and reproducible system. The text articulates six capabilities — Census, Discovery, Development, High Performance, International Acceleration, and Identity — and connects them to twelve quality areas that make it possible to assess where a club generates value, where it loses talent, and what conditions determine its real capacity to convert potential into professional performance.

More than a training manual, the document is a framework for institutional design and audit that combines development methodology, talent identification, human capital, cognitive and physical care, competition, infrastructure, connectivity, and measurement mechanisms.

Download PDF
The Mexican Paradox

"The Mexican Paradox" examines an apparent contradiction in Mexican football: some clubs have academies with development structures comparable to international benchmarks, yet that excellence does not always translate into an equivalent dominance for their first teams. Drawing on an institutional assessment that evaluates twenty academies and cross-references their structural quality (QA Score) with their recent competitive performance (U5A Yield), the study demonstrates that developing talent and converting it into professional performance are distinct capabilities.

The research proposes that the main challenge does not necessarily lie in improving the academy, but in closing the gap between development and competition through greater competitive density, progressive transitions, mobility, and better intermediate structures.

Download PDF
The Paper Academy

"The Paper Academy" raises a fundamental strategic question for any football club: how able would it be to compete if it stopped depending on the transfer market tomorrow? Starting from this question, the document analyzes player development not as a regulatory obligation or a simple cost-saving mechanism, but as strategic infrastructure capable of generating talent, economic value, optionality, financial resilience, identity, human capital, and sustainable competitive advantage.

The difference between a "paper academy" and a true institutional academy is not defined by what a club announces today, but by the capabilities it decides to build and sustain over the next five, ten, or twenty years.

The Talent Refinery

"The Talent Refinery" raises a central question: why does a country with an enormous football culture, a large player base, and a consolidated professional infrastructure fail to systematically convert that potential into world-class talent? The document argues that the problem does not lie solely with the players, the coaches, or the national teams, but in the design of the system that produces them.

The proposal articulates a long-term architecture based on national identification, standardized development, multidimensional evaluation, longitudinal learning, progressive competition, professional acceleration, and institutional governance. Its underlying thesis is that a national team cannot sustainably exceed the talent ceiling produced by its system.

Download PDF
From Scarcity to Competitive Abundance

"From Scarcity to Competitive Abundance" raises a different question about the development of Mexican football: not how to find the next great player, but how to build a system capable of producing elite talent repeatably. The text argues that the problem lies not only in individual quality, but in the distribution of talent, competitive density, the transitions where potential is lost, and the incentives that determine how players progress within the system.

It proposes a national talent-production architecture — the Refinery — organized around six functions: Census, Recruit, Train, Elite-ify, Accelerate, and Identity. Its central thesis is that Mexico does not need only better players, but enough good players for competition among them to systematically push the best toward extraordinary levels.

The Engineering of Conversion

"The Engineering of Conversion" proposes a new way of understanding performance in national-team football: talent, resources, and potential do not by themselves guarantee results. Its central thesis distinguishes between the Structural Ceiling — the capacity a nation can build over the long term — and Situational Conversion — the capacity to transform that potential into competitive performance when it truly matters.

Beyond tactics, the work proposes an institutional architecture for converting the learning of each cycle into accumulable competitive intelligence. The document argues that competitive advantage does not depend solely on having more talent, but on reducing losses, preserving optionality, adapting without losing identity, and building an institution capable of systematically converting its potential into performance.

Comprehensive System of Competitive Robustness

The "Comprehensive System of Competitive Robustness (SIRC)" proposes a paradigm shift in how National Teams' high performance is understood: success does not depend solely on available talent or tactical quality, but on an organization's capacity to convert that potential into stable performance under conditions of maximum pressure, uncertainty, and reduced margin for error.

More than a set of sporting tools, the SIRC proposes an institutional transformation: measuring what precedes the result, converting data into decisions, turning every experience into learning, and preserving that knowledge beyond coaches, generations, or administrations.

The Architecture of Advantage

"The Architecture of Advantage" proposes a framework for understanding why some sporting institutions achieve sustained superiority while others, despite having resources, talent, or one-off successes, fail to reproduce them over time. The text argues that institutional greatness cannot be explained by isolated variables such as budget, coach, or player quality, but by the interaction between identity, strategy, competitive capacity, and the conversion of potential into performance.

Building on this premise, the document develops a systemic view of the club as an architecture of interdependent capabilities, identifies the performance leaks that prevent all available potential from being converted into results, and analyzes the learning, adaptation, and renewal mechanisms that allow an advantage to be sustained against rivals who are also evolving.

Download PDF
The Architecture of Advantage — Volume II

"The Architecture of Advantage — Volume II" explores what makes it possible for a competitive advantage to outlive the people who built it. The text argues that winning for a generation does not, on its own, prove a sustainable advantage: true superiority lies in building an architecture capable of preserving, transmitting, and regenerating an organization's cultural-competitive core —its standards, behaviors under pressure, tacit knowledge, and identity— across successive generations.

The volume examines how that core is transmitted and erodes, the role of "DNA guardians," and why the architect's succession is an institution's moment of greatest risk. Through a contrast between cases such as Bayern Munich and Manchester United, it proposes moving from advantage as something one possesses to a more demanding idea: an advantage that renews itself.

Tigres Eterno: Capacity, Conversion and Archetype

"Tigres Eterno: Capacity, Conversion and Archetype" is a work of institutional diagnosis and transformation that starts from an essential question: how can an institution that reached an extraordinary era of success preserve its greatness once the generation that made it possible is gone? Drawing on the case of Tigres, the text argues that the challenge is not merely to recover sporting results, but to convert existing assets — identity, brand, fanbase, capital, infrastructure, and talent — into an institutional architecture capable of producing competitiveness consistently and sustainably.

The work proposes a comprehensive vision connecting identity, competitive capacity, performance conversion, leadership, governance, academy, learning, capital, ecosystem, and measurement, with a roadmap toward 2035.

Rayados: The Second Founding

"Rayados: The Second Founding" offers a reflection on what a sporting institution needs to transcend the cycles of people, seasons, and results, and become a structurally superior organization. Its central thesis rests on a fundamental distinction between competitive capacity — what an institution is capable of achieving — and competitive conversion — its ability to transform that potential into real performance — and it develops an architecture built on five interdependent capabilities: Capital, Talent, Intelligence, Platform, and Institution.

The true ambition of the "Second Founding" is not merely to win more, but to build an institution capable of learning, reinventing itself, and sustaining its competitiveness as its leaders, coaches, and generations of players change.

Divisional Pyramid of Mexican Football
2 papers
The Parallel Structure

"The Parallel Structure" argues that the main problems of Mexican professional football are not solely sporting in nature, but fundamentally institutional and structural. Starting from the closure of the promotion-and-relegation pyramid, the text proposes rethinking the architecture of national football: creating a parallel, independent, and sustainable structure that restores sporting merit, develops talent, strengthens clubs, and expands the presence of professional football across different regions of the country.

The proposal for a Mexican Football Union (UMF) seeks to build that ecosystem from the bottom up, through a club-based organization, independent governance, transparent rules, comprehensive player development, financial discipline, shared infrastructure, and its own commercial model.

Download PDF
The Governance File

"The Governance File" analyzes the institutional architecture of Mexican football from a legal, economic, and governance perspective, and argues that the system's main challenge lies not only in Liga MX's growing autonomy and economic power, but in the absence of checks and balances capable of preventing that concentration of power from becoming a structural capture of the decisions that affect Mexican federated football as a whole.

The file proposes a progressive strategy that distinguishes between challenging specific sporting decisions and correcting the structures that produce them, articulating a framework for legal and procedural action alongside a structural reform based on five pillars: redistribution of internal power, functional separation between the Federation and the League, protection of the National Team, recovery of the custodial function over the entire football pyramid, and the establishment of external checks and balances.

Strategic Proposals at the Confederation Level
1 paper
Toward a New Architecture for Football in the Americas

"Toward a New Architecture for Football in the Americas" offers a strategic response to the change international football is undergoing following the expansion of the World Cup to 48 national teams. The document argues that the declining function of traditional qualifiers opens a historic opportunity for CONCACAF and CONMEBOL to build a pan-American competitive architecture, without giving up their autonomy or historic competitions.

The proposal is built around three pillars —a Panamerican Nations League, a renewed Copa América, and a Copa Libertadores – Final 8— designed to raise the competitive level, international exposure, and commercial value of continental football. Rather than adding tournaments, it seeks to connect markets, calendars, and sports properties, preserving both confederations' historical identity and adapting to a 48- or eventual 64-team World Cup.

Professional Footballer Operating System
7 papers
From Representation to Governance — Volume I

"From Representation to Governance" —Volume I of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— revisits the traditional model of athletic representation. It starts from a central premise: when the transaction —contract, transfer, or renewal— becomes the industry's primary unit of value, incentives tend to privilege the short term over the athlete's trajectory. Against this, it proposes moving from representing opportunities to directing careers, treating the athlete as a longitudinal trajectory requiring strategy and integral protection.

More than expanding services, it lays out a governance architecture built on uncomfortable truth-telling, diagnosis before sale, and the active protection of athletes during their most vulnerable stages. The proposal redefines what it means to create value: not maximizing transactions, but the quality, resilience, and sustainability of the entire career, through decisions and incentives designed for the long term.

Download PDF
The Stewardship of Talent — Volume II

"The Stewardship of Talent" —Volume II of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— revisits how youth talent development is understood and managed. It starts from a decisive premise: initial talent explains little about a career's ultimate destination. Between identifying a promising young player and their professional consolidation lies a trajectory shaped by the quality of the improvement process, access to real competition, and the stability of the factors surrounding the player.

The text proposes moving from guidance based on intuition toward a measurable, preventive, and transferable development system, able to identify gaps and periodically review each player's evolution. Without replacing human judgment, it seeks to convert accumulated knowledge into a governance architecture that protects and maximizes the odds that potential becomes a sustainable professional trajectory.

The Art of Capitalizing on the Peak — Volume III

"The Art of Capitalizing on the Peak" —Volume III of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— addresses the governance of the established professional career: reaching professionalism does not eliminate career direction, but transforms it into identifying, managing, and capitalizing on value already developed. The work examines silent failure: the loss of high-value opportunities through an inability to recognize and act on a critical window in the trajectory.

It proposes a diagnostic model distinguishing three stages —ascent, peak, and decline or cycle closure— each with its own strategic mode, developing timing and external optionality as value levers, alongside the contractual, sporting, patrimonial, reputational, and post-football dimensions. Altogether, it transforms career management from a reactive practice into a discipline of strategic governance.

The Independent Scaffolding — Volume IV

"The Independent Scaffolding" —Volume IV of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— addresses a frequent error in expanding career-direction systems: assuming a model that works in one market can transplant intact into another by simply changing its language. It distinguishes demographic from structural differences, showing that when a market's physics changes —contractual cycles, protection, supply and demand— expansion requires diagnosis, not just surface adaptation.

It proposes a methodological architecture for expanding without destroying value: diagnosing before deciding, identifying the root cause of failures, and recalibrating parameters to the new context. The result is a third way between copying the original model and reinventing it from scratch. Real expansion capacity lies in building a system solid enough in its principles and adaptable to each market.

Beyond the Field of Play — Volume V

"Beyond the Field of Play" —Volume V of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— examines the extra-competitive environment as a determining variable in an athlete's trajectory and the sustainability of their value. It challenges the view of these domains as "additional services" and treats them as governance infrastructure to reduce friction, anticipate risk, protect assets, and manage the athlete's image and public exposure.

It develops a progressive, evidence-triggered governance model in which defensive and offensive functions —protection and monetization— operate in coordination. It incorporates legitimate activation, tiered escalation, early trust investment, and preparation for the post-competitive transition, under the principle that intervention must respond to a demonstrable need, not a sales opportunity, preserving the athlete's autonomy.

Architecture of Trust — Volume VI

"Architecture of Trust" —Volume VI of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— examines footballers' international mobility from a transaction-governance lens: when a reasonable sporting fit exists, a deal's failure does not stem from lack of talent but from structural frictions of trust, attribution of value, communication, and execution. It identifies these friction points and proposes a common framework applicable to different intermediation configurations.

It develops the framework through a minimum contractual protocol, governance mechanisms during execution, fit-diagnosis criteria, and communication and follow-up systems, treating narrative as an instrument for communicating a player's economic value. It also addresses multilateral coordination, aiming to turn individual deals into a cumulative, replicable institutional capability.

The Economics of Trust — Volume VII

"The Economics of Trust" —Volume VII of the Professional Footballer Operating System Series— examines how an organization converts its principles of integrity and prioritization of the athlete's interest into an institutional architecture that withstands commercial pressure. Drawing on incentives, points of maximum temptation, and quantified guardrails, it proposes moving from trust based on statements to trust backed by structural, verifiable, auditable mechanisms.

It develops the construction of defensible competitive advantages —methodological, relational, integrative, and reputational— and the sequencing discipline needed to build them without overpromising. The result: an organizational architecture where trust ceases to be only an ethical value and becomes, through consistent accumulation, a strategic asset and a competitive barrier that is hard to replicate.

I
Rules and Incentives of Football
1 paper
Breaking Anti-Football

"Breaking Anti-Football" argues that contemporary elite football's problem is not a lack of attacking talent, but an incentive architecture that, under certain circumstances, makes prolonged passivity rational. Drawing on game theory and comparative evidence, it identifies how the current system lets the scoreless draw function as a safety floor that lowers the strategic cost of forgoing offensive initiative.

It proposes a structural redesign with two mechanisms: eliminating the competitive value of the 0-0 scoreline through a scoring reform, and introducing a progressive cost to passivity through a power-play triggered by a prolonged absence of offensive actions. The combination can shift the match's equilibrium toward sustained threat generation, preserving defense's legitimacy, with application criteria, technological safeguards, and gradual, evidence-based adoption.
